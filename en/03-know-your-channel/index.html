Know your channel

Know your channel before you plug in.

▶  Present the slides

When you fly FPV alone, you can pick any video channel you like. The moment you fly with others, one rule matters more than any other: know your channel, and never plug in a battery on a channel someone else is using.

Your drone is a tiny TV station

The video transmitter on your drone broadcasts its camera picture on a radio frequency around 5.8 GHz. Your goggles tune in to that frequency, like a radio tuned to a station. Most analog FPV gear uses Raceband: eight channels, R1 to R8, spaced 37 MHz apart. One channel, one pilot.

What goes wrong

Imagine Karl is racing on R3. Mia, on the bench, plugs a battery into her drone, which is also set to R3. Her transmitter is right next to Karl's goggles, much closer than his drone, so it wins. Suddenly Karl isn't seeing the track any more; he's seeing Mia's workbench, or just static. At 100 km/h, that's a crash.

That's why this is the number one rule. Almost every other mistake in FPV costs you something. This one costs someone else, and they did nothing wrong.

The power-up ritual

At HOBIzone we use an electronic channel reservation screen. Every time, before every battery:

1. Register: go to the screen and book your channel. 2. Wait: if someone else is already registered on your channel, wait your turn. 3. Shout: "Plugging in on R3!" 4. Plug in: at 25 mW. Never more, in practice or in a race. 5. Check: pilots in the air confirm their video is still clean. 6. Fly.

Quiet and fair

How loud you transmit matters as much as where. Pit mode (well under 1 mW) is not a free pass: it protects pilots in the air from a drone on the ground, but it still reaches the pilot next to you, so don't power up at the bench while others fly. In the air, 25 mW is plenty for any track, and at HOBIzone it's the limit, in practice and in races. More power doesn't make your own picture much better; it mostly drowns out everyone else's.

What about DJI? Digital systems like DJI play by different rules. Their signal is much wider than an analog channel, so it covers more of the band, and the goggles transmit too. We don't race with DJI, but if someone brings one to practice, book it on the screen like everyone else and keep a little distance from the analog pilots.

Flying side by side is the best part of this hobby. It only works when everyone can trust the person next to them.
