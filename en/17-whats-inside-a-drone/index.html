What’s inside a drone

Learn one drone, and you know them all.

▶  Present the slides

A whoop weighs about 20 grams and fits in your hand. Our 5″ kit drone is close to 300 grams and fast enough to scare you. They look nothing alike, but open them up and you'll find the same things inside. This guide goes through them, using the drone we build in the club as the example.

Same recipe, two sizes

Every FPV drone has eight ingredients: a frame, motors and props, an ESC, a flight controller, a receiver, a camera, a video transmitter and a battery. On our 5″ each part sits on its own board. On a whoop the frame is plastic with ducts round the props, and four of the parts (ESC, flight controller, receiver and video transmitter) are squeezed onto one board. Same recipe, smaller kitchen.

Three paths

Every part sits on one of three paths:

  • Control: your radio, the receiver, the flight controller, the ESC, the motors. Your sticks become motor speed.
  • Video: the camera, the video transmitter, its antenna, your goggles. What the drone sees, you see.
  • Power: the battery feeds the ESC and the motors. The flight controller passes power on to the camera, the video transmitter and the receiver.

When something doesn't work, ask which path it's on. That tells you where to look.

The frame

Our kit drone's frame comes as a box of flat carbon parts: four arms, two protection bars, a bottom, middle and top plate, four aluminium columns, a soft fin that holds the antennas, a camera mount and a bag of screws. The arms sit between the bottom and middle plate, so a broken arm is quick to swap. It's 195 mm from one motor to the opposite one, and all four arms are the same: a true X.

Motors and props

Brushless motors spin the props. The bell on top spins, with the prop on its shaft, and the base is screwed to the arm. Each motor has three wires to the ESC. Two props spin one way and two the other, so the drone doesn't turn round on its own. Our kit drone has 2306.5 motors: the stator inside is 23 mm wide and 6.5 mm tall. They're 1900 KV, which means about 1900 rpm for every volt. On a full 6S pack that's close to 48,000 rpm without a prop. The props are 5″ three-blades. A whoop has tiny motors, about 7 or 8 mm, and 31 mm props inside the ducts.

ESC

A brushless motor needs its three wires switched on and off in turn, very fast. The ESC (electronic speed controller) does that for each motor, as fast as the flight controller tells it, thousands of times a second. Ours has four ESCs on one board, 60 A for each motor, with a capacitor on the battery pads to soak up electrical spikes. On a whoop a small ESC sits on the same board as everything else.

Flight controller

The brain. The gyro feels every tilt and turn. The processor reads the gyro and your sticks and works out how fast each motor should spin, thousands of times a second. It runs Betaflight, and you set it up from a computer over USB. Ours has an F722 processor, an ICM42688P gyro, a barometer and a 16 MB black box that records every flight. It also gives 5 V to the other parts.

Receiver

Your radio sends your sticks at 2.4 GHz. The receiver picks them up and passes them to the flight controller. It only listens to the radio it's bound to, so ten pilots can fly at the same time. Most drones have a separate receiver: a tiny ExpressLRS board, about 10 mm, with four wires (5 V, ground, RX and TX) and a T-antenna at the back, away from the carbon. Our kit drone's flight controller has one built in, and so does a whoop's board.

Camera

Your eyes. The camera turns the view into a video signal for the video transmitter. It sits at the front, tilted up, so when the drone leans forward to fly fast you still see ahead. Ours is a Foxeer Predator 5 Nano: 14 mm, with a 1.7 mm lens, a 155° view and about 4 ms of delay.

Video transmitter and antenna

The video transmitter (VTX) turns the camera picture into a 5.8 GHz radio signal, and the antenna sends it out to your goggles. The antenna is a part of its own, with its own plug. Never power a VTX without its antenna on: it can burn out. Ours can send at 25 to 350 mW on 40 channels and has a microphone built in. We race at 25 mW. The antenna sits in the fin at the back.

Battery

A LiPo battery feeds the ESC and the motors, and through the flight controller everything else. The thick leads with the XT60 plug carry the power. The thin balance lead lets the charger look after each cell. Our kit drone flies on 6S, six cells and 22.2 V, with 1300 to 1500 mAh, strapped on top. A whoop flies on 1S, one cell, under the frame. How batteries work and how to keep them safe is in talks 14 and 15.

All together

Seen side by side at the same scale, the electronics are small. Hold a part up next to the picture: every pad has a job.

Where everything goes

From the side you can see how it all fits. The stack sits in the middle, between the plates: the ESC at the bottom, the flight controller above it and the video transmitter on top. The camera is at the front. The antennas are at the back, away from the props. The battery is strapped on top. Heavy parts stay close to the centre and the wires stay short.

Learn one, know them all

Small or big, the ingredients don't change. Only how many there are, and how they're packed:

  • 65 mm whoop: four parts on one board.
  • 5″ racer, like our kit drone: a stack, with the ESC under the flight controller.
  • 7″ long range: often a separate ESC on each arm.
  • Cinelifter: a drone that carries a big camera, with 8 motors, two on each arm, and 8 ESCs.

So once you know what's inside one drone, you can find your way around any of them.
